<h2 class="research-title">Language and the human brain</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Understanding speech feels effortless, yet within a fraction of a second the brain must turn a continuous stream of sound into words and meaning. In <a href="{{ site.lab.url }}">Dr. Christian Brodbeck's lab</a>, I study how this unfolds by recording magnetoencephalography (MEG) while people listen to natural, continuous speech. Using neural source current response functions (NCRFs), I model how acoustic and linguistic features of the speech drive activity across specific regions of cortex over time, revealing where and when the brain processes sounds, words, and meaning.</p>

<h2 class="research-title">Language, action, and robotics</h2>
<p class="research-desc">My doctoral research studied how embodied language develops in robotic systems. I developed a neural network architecture based on the Free Energy Principle, trained with multimodal data from an arm robot, and studied how learned behavior generalizes to novel contexts.</p>
